# Worked Example — Southern Utah + Northern Arizona

An illustrative example of how the planner's calibration and cut decisions work. The group details are representative, not a real family.

## Group
A family group with elementary-age children, possibly joined by a second family; scenic, family-friendly trip; normally easy/moderate hikes with an approximate 4-mile ceiling; relaxed evenings.

## Priorities
Zion/Narrows; Bryce hike; slot canyon (non-negotiable); Grand Canyon Railway; Grand Canyon eastern viewpoints; Coral Pink Sand Dunes; The Wave if lottery; intentional stargazing.

## Key calibration lesson
The Narrows was initially considered as a 4–5 hour outing. Questioning exposed that river hiking involves cold water, slippery footing and greater fatigue than an equivalent dry trail. The locked plan became approximately 3–4 hours, roughly 2–3 river miles, with a flexible turnaround and a shorter 2.5–3 hour version if water is especially cold/high.

General lesson: before using a user-stated activity duration, determine what terrain and conditions that duration assumes.

## Key planning lessons
- Protect non-negotiables; Horseshoe Bend became cut-first.
- Give lottery-dependent activities a real alternative; The Wave's fallback became Kolob Canyons + Taylor Creek.
- Differentiate consecutive days rather than repeating the same experience.
- Classify park admission separately from activity-specific fees.
- Produce both a detailed Master Trip Plan and a concise graphical group-text version.
- After travel, separate trip-specific observations, group calibration and reusable methodology lessons.
